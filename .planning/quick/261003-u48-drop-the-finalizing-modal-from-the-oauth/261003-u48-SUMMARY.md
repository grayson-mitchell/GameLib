## D-1: full provenance for the amended discriminator (read standalone, no re-derivation needed)

The original quick task 261003-s04 established D-3's discriminator as "is a native sign-in window on screen?" — a Dialog rendered for every windowless wait (`preparing`, `finalizing`) plus the three failure phases, and rendered nothing when a window was up or during `idle`.

This task (261003-u48) **amends** that D-3, rather than predating or replacing it outright. The amended discriminator is narrower: "is a native window on screen, OR is the windowless wait long enough that a 500ms-transition Dialog is worth the screen space it takes?" The operator measured, on this machine, on 2026-10-03: ~1-2s for GOG's post-window exchange and ~4-5s for Amazon's post-window exchange — both short enough that against `Dialog`'s own 500ms `transitionDuration`, the modal spent most of its mounted life sliding in and back out rather than holding still with visible content.

The superseded figure the original D-3 reasoning cited for this phase — "5-27s" — appears **exactly once** in the whole repo, attributed to quick task 260803-eee, and names no specific runner. It does not describe GOG or Amazon specifically, and the operator's own fresh, named measurement supersedes it here.

`preparing` was deliberately left untouched and remains in the Dialog set: it IS measured, at ~12.8s, and is Amazon-only (the nile-auth subprocess spawn, quick task 260806-teb) — long enough that the modal's content is genuinely visible rather than mostly transitioning, unlike `finalizing`.

The feedback for the now-silenced wait does not disappear — it moves to the clicked tile's busy spinner on the Accounts screen (`Runner/index.tsx`). The `/loginweb/<runner>` route is untouched by this amendment: it still mounts `TauriLoginPanel` directly (not through this overlay) and still renders that component's `finalizing` branch in full — no copy and no catalogue key died; only this overlay stopped wrapping that branch in a Dialog.

- **Fix:** Reworded to `` `/loginweb/<runner>` `` — eliminates the literal `/*` substring while preserving the same meaning. Re-ran the Node.js simulation to confirm `'preparing'` and `new Set([` both resolve to valid indices in the stripped output, with `'finalizing'` and `'awaiting'` both correctly absent.
