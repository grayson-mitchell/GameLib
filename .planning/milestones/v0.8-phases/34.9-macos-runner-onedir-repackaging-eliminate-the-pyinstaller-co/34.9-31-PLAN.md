THE SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT REQUIREMENT IN THIS CONTRACT.

Direction C re-runs the reviewer's concurrency harness against the new wrapper and expects ZERO
cross-contamination. But zero cross-contamination is exactly what a BROKEN HARNESS also reports. A
harness that silently stopped measuring anything -- wrong path, race window closed by a timing
change, comparison always true -- would produce a perfect result and prove nothing.

So Direction C is invalid without a POSITIVE CONTROL: the same harness, in the same session, on the
same hardware, pointed at the OLD shared-outfile idiom, must still reproduce cross-contamination at
the rate previously measured (reviewer 60/60, verifier 20/20, both 100%). Only a harness that goes
RED on the old idiom and GREEN on the new one has measured anything at all.

This is this project's hardest-won standing rule -- a guard must be proven to FAIL against a
known-bad input -- applied to a measurement harness rather than an assertion. `34.9-PIPE-PROOF.md`
records the same idea as its "A0 counterfactual"; read that section and its honest status note
before writing Direction C.
</positive_control>
