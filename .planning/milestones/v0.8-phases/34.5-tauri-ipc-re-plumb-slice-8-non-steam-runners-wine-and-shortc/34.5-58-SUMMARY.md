## What the session found

Two launches, real `keyring` arm both times (`secret stores: keyring` = 1, `[dev-secret-vault]` = 0),
operator present, screen unlocked, `In dark wake` = 0.

**Launch 1 — read FAILED with no UI at all.** `humble-session` hung the full bound and gave up:
`keyring:timeout`, `elapsed=45012ms`, and the operator observed **zero dialogs**. Sign-out then drove
one `keyring_delete ok` and one delete that **reported failure but actually succeeded**. Re-login
wrote both slots and the following read was cache-served.

**Launch 2 — read SUCCEEDED.** `keyring_get ok present=true len=208` (12860ms) and `len=29`
(6623ms), both `len` matching the bytes written at 21:13:25, so genuine secret-bearing reads rather
than empty successes. Zero timeouts.

## Honesty section

- **`U-34.5-01`'s retirement rests entirely on launch 2.** Launch 1 produced no successful read. A
  single successful trial retires the row on its own stated bar — that bar asks for *at least one*
  successful read — but it is one trial, and the same slot failed 11 minutes earlier.
- **The ACL hypothesis is unproven.** Launch 2 succeeded after both items were re-created by the
  running binary, which is consistent with macOS granting the creating binary ACL access, but one
  trial does not establish the mechanism. A rebuild changing the binary signature is the real test
  and was not run.
- **`F-34.5-G6-28`'s 2:1 ratio is unexplained**, not merely unattributed.
- **I withdrew my own pigeonhole argument** for `U-34.5-10` mid-session. It concluded the row FAILS
  from "4 prompts across 2 slots", which assumed per-item attribution that turned out not to exist.
- **I also corrected myself on "45 seconds was not enough."** With no UI presented, no timeout value
  would have helped — a different defect than the one 34.5-35 targeted.
