<objective>
Collapse the preload surface: unify the two divergent secret policies onto the fail-closed
allow-list, remove the `isTauri()` Electron branches and the lazy `electron-store` require from
`misc.ts`, kill the four lazy `require('electron')` calls in `ipc.ts`, and give every bare
`Electron.` namespace reference an explicit first-party type.

Purpose: two of the three things this plan does are invisible to the grep that D-03's success test
uses. A mechanical sed over `from 'electron'` completes neither — `Electron.IpcRendererEvent` has no
import statement at all, and `require('electron')` is not an import statement. Both would survive
untouched into a build where the package is gone.

And `preload/api/misc.ts` is the phase's highest-risk file: three independent Phase 35 concerns
physically overlap in one ~70-line region, accreted across Phases 28, 29 and 34.x with nothing
forcing them into shared awareness until now. `35-PATTERNS.md` calls it the highest-risk file for a
silent merge conflict. It is handled in one task, deliberately.

The security half has a strict order. The Electron path's deny-list is being deleted in favour of
the Tauri path's allow-list; `misc.ts`'s own comment says "the two builds deliberately carry
divergent secret policies until the Electron cutover (Phase 35)... Do not 'fix' this by unifying the
two policies early; that is Phase 35's job." Unifying means proving the allow-list already blocks
everything the deny-list blocked — before, not after.

Output: one secret policy, proven subsuming; zero `isTauri()` in `misc.ts`; zero lazy electron
requires; every `Electron.` reference replaced by a named import.
</objective>
