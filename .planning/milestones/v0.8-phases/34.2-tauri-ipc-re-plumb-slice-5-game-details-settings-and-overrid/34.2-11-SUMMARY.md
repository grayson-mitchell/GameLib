## Decisions Made

- **Prose correction over a "real" transitive gate.** The plan's own measurement (reproduced here, matching exactly) shows a genuinely transitive purity gate would require a 29-module allowlist spanning nearly the entire backend — including `constants/paths.ts`, `common/types.ts`, and `utils.ts`, which almost every file imports. Such a gate would constrain nothing and would itself be a lie by omission. Correcting the prose and committing the measurement as a monotonic ledger converts an overclaim into an honest, useful artifact without pretending to solve Phase 35's real problem (untangling the backend's electron coupling) inside a gap cycle.
- **Growth-only (subset), not strict-equality.** Phase 35 will remove entries from this set over time as it decouples modules from `electron`; a strict-equality pin would go red on every legitimate improvement. The tripwire only fails when the measured set contains something NOT in the baseline — i.e., when reach GROWS.
- **Baseline regenerated, not pasted.** The plan explicitly required the baseline be regenerated from the real resolver rather than copied from planning-time prose, and to record any difference rather than force agreement. The regenerated baseline's electron-importing set (29 files) is byte-identical to the planning-time list. The total graph size differs slightly (192 vs. the planning-time note's 194) — see Issues Encountered below.

## Issues Encountered

- **192 vs. 194 total first-party files.** The plan's `<interfaces>` section recorded a planning-time measurement of 194 first-party files in the transitive graph, 29 of which import `electron` directly. Re-running the resolver at execution time (fresh code, not copy-pasted) measured **192** first-party files — the load-bearing number (29 electron-importing files) is byte-identical to the planning-time list, but the total graph size is 2 files smaller. The plan explicitly anticipated this class of discrepancy ("if it differs... record the difference and the reason... rather than forcing either value"). The most likely explanation is a difference in how the planning-time measurement counted `.json`/`.d.ts` resolution targets or entry-point self-counting at its own tool's edges; since the property this ledger enforces (the electron-importing set) is unaffected, and the `>100` reachability-sanity assertion holds comfortably either way (192 ≫ 100), this was not investigated further per the plan's own guidance not to force either value.
