## How the register was built, and why the unit matters

**62 audit units.** The unit is `(plan, threat_id)`, never `threat_id` alone.

The numbered IDs `T-34.4-01` … `T-34.4-52` are unique — no ID appears in two plans. But every one
of the 10 plans also carries its own **`T-34.4-SC`** supply-chain row, and those are **10 distinct
rows sharing one ID**. Each makes a plan-specific claim: most assert "this plan installs no
packages", 34.4-05's additionally asserts `node:sea` is a Node builtin, 34.4-08's additionally
asserts `src-tauri/` is byte-unchanged, 34.4-09's asserts its gate script uses only the Python
standard library. They are verified separately and counted separately.

**A first census of this register returned 52 and was wrong.** The regex used required a trailing
digit (`T-34\.4-[A-Za-z0-9-]*\d+`), which silently dropped all ten `T-34.4-SC` rows — a **16%
undercount**, and one that would have produced a clean-looking audit with ten supply-chain claims
never examined. Corrected before any shard was dispatched. This is the fifth recorded instance in
this project of an audit run at a coarser unit than the defect it was meant to find.

| Shard | Plans | Rows | Closed | Open |
|-------|-------|------|--------|------|
| A | 34.4-01 … 34.4-05 | 31 | 31 | 0 |
| B | 34.4-06 … 34.4-10 | 31 | 30 | 1 |
| **Total** | **10** | **62** | **61** | **1** |

Arithmetic: `31 + 31 = 62` rows; `31 + 30 = 61` closed; `62 − 61 = 1` open.

Shard B returned **26 closed / 5 open**. Four of its five open rows were closed by the
orchestrator *after* the shard reported — two by repairing a broken control, two by establishing
facts the shard did not have. Those four are documented individually below rather than silently
folded into the total.

## The four rows shard B reported OPEN that are now CLOSED

Recorded individually because "the auditor said open, the orchestrator said closed" is exactly the
shape that should never be silently absorbed into a total.

### T-34.4-41 and T-34.4-42 — CLOSED by repairing a control that broke *the same day*

**Shard B was right, and the finding was serious.** `ported-channels-gate.py`'s declared mitigation
is that every `check_*` function has a matching self-test feeding a synthetic violation through the
same function — the anti-vacuity control for the whole gate. Measured directly:
`python3 ported-channels-gate.py --self-test` **exited 1**, aborting at
`self-test setup FAILED: the base synthetic inventory document does not pass a clean gate run`
**before any of its 9 cases ran**. The declared control was dead.

**Root cause, and it is a same-day regression, not old rot.** Commit `e33dc2744` — *"fix(planning):
repair the two planning gates that were silently red"*, landed 2026-08-23 — moved the slice-8
heading assertion from `(57 channels)` to `(58 channels)` in the live regex and its error message,
but left the **two synthetic fixtures** at `(57 channels)` (lines 436 and 452). Repairing one gate
broke another gate's self-test, within hours, in the same file.

**Fix applied:** both fixtures updated `57` → `58`.

**The second fixture had to change for a different reason than the first, and this is the part that
matters.** Fixture 1 (`_valid_synthetic_inventory_doc`) is the *known-good* base — it must pass a
clean run or setup aborts. Fixture 2 (`_synthetic_38_channel_inventory_doc`) is the *known-bad*
document that case 9 feeds to `check_inventory_slice8_intact` to prove it rejects a reverted scope
split. Had fixture 2 been left at `57`, case 9 would still have "passed" — but by failing on the
**missing heading** rather than on the missing `isLoggedIn`. A case passing for the wrong reason is
a vacuous discriminator, the precise defect these self-tests exist to prevent.

**Verified after the fix, in both directions:**
- `python3 ported-channels-gate.py --self-test` → **exit 0**, `All 9 check_* function(s) proved
  capable of rejecting the input they exist to reject (9 self-test case(s), 1:1)`.
- `python3 ported-channels-gate.py` (main mode) → **exit 0** against the real documents.
- **Non-vacuity of the repair itself, proven on a scratch copy:** adding `isLoggedIn` to fixture 2's
  slice-8 list made case 9 stop rejecting, and the harness correctly reported
  `self-test FAILED: ... did NOT reject its bad input — gate is vacuous` (exit 1). Case 9 therefore
  discriminates on the `isLoggedIn` property, not on the heading literal that was changed.

**Method note worth keeping.** The first exit-code reading here was taken as
`python3 ... | tail -20; echo $?`, which captures **`tail`'s** status, not the gate's, and reported
a false `0`. Re-measured without the pipe. A gate's exit code must be read unpiped.

### T-34.4-48 — CLOSED as a form gap, with the mechanism deliberately left unused

Shard B correctly observed that all five D-09 precondition checkboxes are `[ ]` and were never
ticked, against an acceptance criterion demanding all five ticked in order.

**They remain unticked on purpose.** Ticking them on 2026-08-23 would be a present-day hand
asserting a 2026-07-27 observation it did not make, and three of the five are struck as obsolete by
the 2026-07-30 strike — a tick would be actively misleading to a future reader.

The threat is item 4 being **silently skipped because its precondition was unstated**. The
precondition was stated, followed, and narrated *in order* in the item's Evidence line, which names
the Electron login step explicitly. Steps 4–5 are evidenced by the item's own PASS. Mechanism
unused; property on the record; threat did not materialise. An audit note recording exactly this
was added to `34.4-LIVE-GATE.md`.

### T-34.4-51 — CLOSED, but the finding was sharper than the shard stated

Shard B reported this as "the observation was never recorded". **The real defect was worse: the
mitigation was never written at all.** `T-34.4-51`'s declared control is that item 4's instructions
state the D-04 panel is expected and that a blank screen IS a failure. `grep -n "blank"` across the
whole 515-line document returned **zero matches** — that instruction did not exist anywhere.

Two separate remedies, both applied:
1. **The missing instruction is now written into item 4**, so the control exists for any re-run.
   Declared-but-never-written is the same class as phase 34.5's R2, and the precedent there was to
   build the control rather than close the row against absent text.
2. **For the 2026-07-27 run the observation is genuinely NOT APPLICABLE.** That run reached Tauri
   via the Electron-first D-09 detour, so the Humble login screen was rendered only by the
   **Electron** build — where the real webview login works and the D-04 panel cannot appear by
   construction. No Tauri login was attempted, so there was no panel to observe and no blank screen
   to misread. The tester's "not separately recorded" note is over-cautious, not a gap.

## Open Threats — 1 row

### T-34.4-49 — DoS — an irreversible action taken during the live gate (plan 34.4-10)

**Disposition:** mitigate. **Status: OPEN — half-discharged on evidence, half needs one line from
the operator.**

Plan 34.4-10's acceptance criterion is retrospective, not preventive: *"The record confirms that
neither `redeemSteamKey` nor `steamBottleProvision` was run."* The forward instruction exists
(`34.4-LIVE-GATE.md` § D-08, "Do not run either of these two channels during Task 2/3"). The
confirmation does not.

- **`steamBottleProvision` — CONFIRMED NOT RUN.** Established from this gate's own measurement,
  taken for an unrelated purpose: item 5 recorded `steamBottleStatus() → { provisioned: false }` at
  2026-07-27 with the `provisioned` flag **unset** in `steamBottleConfigStore`. That flag is written
  *only* by `provisionBottle()`'s own paths (`bottle.ts:264`, `:671`, `:823`), so an invocation
  during the gate would necessarily have written it. Real proof, not an assertion.
- **`redeemSteamKey` — NOT CONFIRMED EITHER WAY.** No artifact in `34.4-LIVE-GATE.md`,
  `34.4-10-SUMMARY.md` or `34.4-REVIEW.md` records whether it was invoked, and unlike the bottle
  flag there is no incidental on-disk trace to reason from. None of the five gate items exercise it
  and D-08 forbids it — circumstantial, not evidence.

**What would close it:** one line from the operator who ran the 2026-07-27 gate confirming
`redeemSteamKey` was not invoked. **Or** an explicit accepted-risk entry recording that the gate
predates the requirement and the confirmation is unobtainable.

**Deliberately not closed by inference.** The circumstantial case is strong, and writing "no gate
item exercises it, therefore it was not run" would look like evidence while being an argument. A
burned Steam key is irreversible; this row is cheap to close correctly and should be.
