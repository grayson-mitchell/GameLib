### Divider Investigation Arc (first-class finding, not a footnote)

The tier-2 panel's right-edge divider went through **five attempts across two plans** before landing on a mechanism that actually works. Each of the first four was falsified by evidence, not by guesswork discovered later — this is the record of what each one got right and what it missed:

1. **`var(--divider)`** (pre-existing, found during 34.11 UI-SPEC scouting) — Undefined in 9 of 11 theme blocks. An undefined custom property with no fallback drops the *entire* declaration at computed-value time, so the divider was simply absent (not miscoloured) in `gruvbox_dark`, `dracula`, and 7 other themes.
2. **`var(--body-background)`** (plan 34.11-03) — Resolves in all 11 theme blocks, so the declaration never drops. This fixed *resolution* but not *visibility*: `.NavShell__tier2` (the panel, left of the border) paints `var(--navbar-background)`; `.App .content` (the game grid, right of the border) paints `var(--gradient-body-background, var(--body-background))`. In every theme without a `--gradient-body-background` override — midnightMirage included — that resolves to the *exact same* `var(--body-background)` the border used. A border painted the same colour as the surface it abuts on one side is invisible against that side by construction, independent of whether the token resolves. Live sweep (this plan, Task 3, first pass): reported completely absent in midnightMirage.
3. **1px `var(--neutral-05)`** (this plan) — Measured contrast against both neighbours in all three mandatory themes ranged 7.65:1–8.83:1, comfortably clearing WCAG's non-text-UI floor. Still reported completely absent. A pixel-level Playwright/Chromium reproduction of the exact DOM/CSS, using real extracted token values, ruled out colour and occlusion (the line rendered perfectly in Chromium, un-occluded, at DPR 1 and 2) — pointing at the one variable Chromium cannot reproduce: a fractional `width × DPR` CSS Grid track boundary (`.App`'s `grid-template-columns: min-content 1fr`), the same defect class already recorded once as "WKWebView % height vs 1fr grid row."
4. **2px `var(--neutral-05)`** (this plan) — **LIVE-CONFIRMED FIX for the rasterisation gap.** The divider became visible in midnightMirage. This proved the WKWebView-fractional-grid-track hypothesis and, at the time, supported a reasonable inference: **"a 1px hairline on this grid boundary cannot survive in WKWebView; 2px is the floor."** That inference was reasonable given all evidence available at that point — but re-sweep verdict: too heavy, reads as a chunky rule, not the hairline the design specifies. Softened to `--neutral-04` (measurably closer to the design system's "strong border" role than `--neutral-05`'s "muted text"), with a `body.dracula`/`body.dracula-classic` override back to `--neutral-05` because `--navbar-background` (#44475a) and `--neutral-04` (#51595a) share an identical blue channel and rendered with no perceptible line in a pixel-level screenshot of dracula's panel side specifically.
5. **1px `::after` pseudo-element, `var(--neutral-04)` with the same dracula override** (this plan, final) — The human approved trying one specific, previously-untried mechanism: draw the divider as a `position: absolute` pseudo-element positioned against `.NavShell__tier2`'s own settled padding box, instead of a `border` painted as part of CSS Grid item layout at the seam between two independently-rounded quantities (the grid track's own resolved boundary and the item's own border-box edge). **Live-confirmed: 1px renders correctly in all three mandatory themes.** This falsifies attempt 4's "2px is the floor" conclusion outright — the floor was never about pixel count, it was about which rendering subsystem paints the line. Recorded explicitly here because "2px is the floor" was a reasonable, evidence-backed inference at the time it was drawn, and without this record a future contributor could easily re-derive and re-ship it as settled fact.

**The pattern across all five attempts:** the actual defect was only found once a *discriminator* was built (a pixel-level rendering reproduction) instead of substituting another plausible token or width on faith. Items 1–2 were resolution/relational-colour reasoning; item 3 added real, measured contrast and was still wrong; item 4 fixed the true mechanism (rasterisation) but over-corrected on weight; item 5 changed the mechanism family entirely. No single piece of evidence at any intermediate step was dishonest or unmeasured — each was locally correct and still insufficient, which is the specific shape of defect that a checklist of "did you check contrast" or "did you check resolution" cannot catch, and why the investigation escalated to live rendering evidence rather than another round of token substitution.

### Gate Lesson: a source-text gate can stay green while a sibling rule defeats its target

`themeTokens.test.ts`'s assertion for `.NavShell__tier2`'s border-inline-end was **green throughout the entire period the divider was invisible**, and it was **not vacuous** — it correctly proved the source said exactly what it claimed at every step (first `var(--body-background)`, later `var(--neutral-04)`, etc.). The gate was never the problem.

What actually happened: `Header/index.css`'s stale background rule, introduced into this exact DOM subtree by *this plan* (mounting `Header` inside the portal for the first time), painted over the divider from a sibling/descendant position the gate was never written to know about. The gate correctly proved the ONE property it was scoped to (`.NavShell__tier2`'s own `border-inline-end`) was exactly right; a completely different component's rule, added later, defeated its visual purpose without the asserted declaration ever changing.

This is a **distinct failure mode from vacuity**, and it is worth naming precisely because it evades the usual defenses:
- An item-by-item review of "is this assertion vacuous" would have passed every one of these gates, correctly, at every point.
- The fix was not "write a stronger assertion" — the assertion was already as strong as a source-text gate scoped to one declaration can be.
- The fix was structural: removing the *other* rule that was defeating the divider from outside the gate's scope entirely (`Header/index.css`'s background), which a gate scoped to `.NavShell__tier2` could never have caught, since it was never asserting anything about `Header`.
- The only tool that caught it was a live human sweep followed by rendering-level forensics — this is a category of defect that source-text gates in a `testEnvironment: 'node'` project (no jsdom, no CSS engine — see `themeTokens.test.ts`'s own docstring) are structurally unable to see, by design, not by omission.
